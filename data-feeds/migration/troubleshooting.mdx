A short, practical list of the things that most often come up when you start consuming Data Feeds. Each entry
is **symptom, why, and what to do**.

## The sink started but no rows have appeared yet

**Why:** a recipe in `historical` or `hybrid` mode backfills a window of past blocks before (or while) it goes
live, so there is a short warm-up before rows land.

**Do this:** give it a minute, then check the sink is healthy. A healthy sink logs that it has seeded its
cursor near the chain tip, followed by lines showing it fetching block windows that advance forward without
errors. If rows still do not appear, confirm your `MORALIS_CHAIN_ID` and start block are set as you intend.
See [Modes](/data-feeds/concepts/modes).

## The newest rows look duplicated or briefly wrong

**Why:** near the chain tip, a block can be replaced by a reorg. ClickHouse recipes write a **correcting row**
when this happens rather than silently deleting, so a naive `SELECT *` can momentarily show both the original
and its cancel.

**Do this:** read ClickHouse fact tables with **`FINAL`** (or use sign-aware sums for aggregates). That applies
the correction and gives you the canonical result. Settled history is never affected, only the tip. See
[Reorgs](/data-feeds/concepts/reorgs).

## I get a 403 or a 402 from the API

**Why:** they mean different things. **403** means your key is **not scoped to that chain**. **402** means your
**subscription or access is inactive**, an account-level state, not a per-chain one.

**Do this:** for **403**, use a key that includes the chain you are requesting, or request access for it. For
**402**, check your Data Feeds access is active.

## A token has no price

**Why:** prices are **calculated from real onchain DEX trades**, not from a separate pricing engine. A token
that has not traded recently may not have a current price yet.

**Do this:** treat a missing price as "no recent trades" rather than an error, and fall back gracefully. This
is the same reason USD values are a **calculated** value best compared with a small tolerance. See
[Data Feeds vs. the legacy API](/data-feeds/concepts/data-feeds-vs-the-legacy-api).

## My current-state results are incomplete

**Why:** answers like balances and holders are **current state**, the cumulative result of every past event. If
you start indexing too late, you miss anyone whose last relevant activity predates your start block, and the
result is quietly incomplete.

**Do this:** backfill from the beginning of the relevant history, the token's deploy block for by-token
answers, or full history for by-wallet answers. Event-list answers (swaps, transfers, logs) do not have this
constraint. See [History & backfill](/data-feeds/concepts/history-and-backfill).

## Reading the lake directly (REST / Arrow Flight)

If you consume the feed directly instead of through the sink, a few extra things are worth knowing.

### Do not hard-code the feed version

**Why:** each chain's feed carries a version that can roll over when the producer is upgraded, so a hard-coded
version can start returning a lagging tip or stop resolving.

**Do this:** resolve the active topic from the chain's status before reading, and pick the current version
rather than assuming one.

### The most recent blocks are not available as files

**Why:** settled history is stored as downloadable files; the most recent blocks near the tip are served live
and are not yet flushed to file storage.

**Do this:** read recent/near-tip data from the live stream, and use the file path for settled history. The
chain status tells you where that boundary currently sits.

### Older history may be missing newer fields

**Why:** the decoded schema has gained fields over time, so partitions from before a field existed genuinely do
not carry it.

**Do this:** discover the columns actually present for the range you are reading, and treat an absent field as
zero or none rather than a failure.

### Keep direct-read concurrency modest

**Why:** each direct read does real work to prepare the data, so very high concurrency can degrade rather than
cleanly rate-limit.

**Do this:** keep concurrency modest, and add retries with exponential backoff on transient `5xx` responses.

<Note>
**Still stuck?** Most first-run issues come down to the backfill warm-up, the `FINAL` reading rule, or a chain
or access scope mismatch. If you have ruled those out, capture the recipe, chain, and the exact query or
request, and reach out through your Data Feeds support channel.
</Note>
